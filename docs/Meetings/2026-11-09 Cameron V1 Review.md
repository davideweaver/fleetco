## Overall assessment

Build every quote in the new shop app. Keep the sales pipeline and customer communication in
HighLevel. Moving an opportunity to Print Production should create the job from its linked quote. The
shop app handles deposits, production, installation, invoicing and customer balances. Use Box for
production files and Box Sign for signed proofs.

This review includes Cameron's V1 decisions. The items at the end are open for Dave or accounting to
confirm.

# Answers for Dave

## 1. Estimates and approval

**1.3 · Quote approval.** Build and revise customer quotes in the shop app. Export the PDF and send
it through our existing email or HighLevel. In V1, record approval on the quote, including who
approved, when, and which revision and items were approved. Keep the quote linked to the HighLevel
opportunity so its approved items are available at the job handoff. One-click quote approval can
wait.

**1.4 · Collecting deposits in V1.** V1 must collect deposits. From a quote, let me set the deposit
amount, generate a Stripe payment link and share it through HighLevel or email. Use the usual 50%
when our policy calls for it and allow approved account terms or exceptions. Record check, cash and
other payments received outside the link. Show the amount due and the amount paid. Keep the deposit
linked to the quote and customer account so it follows the job and reduces the correct final invoice
once. Keep the $149 design deposit separately identifiable. Printing stays blocked until a required
deposit is satisfied.

**1.5 · Vehicle and non-vehicle work.** Let us quote, sell and invoice work without a vehicle.
Examples include signs, walls, windows, decals and vinyl sold by itself. A vehicle, trailer or site
can be attached when relevant. Capture the site address and notes when needed. A VIN or installation
appointment should be optional. Carry the customer PO through to the invoice and let us use separate
project and billing contacts where needed.

**1.1–1.2 · Quote builder and layout.** Use the saved packages shown in the guide as a starting point
for building quotes in the shop app. Let us add or edit labor, materials, products, quantities and
prices, including simple decal and vinyl sales. Keep internal costs and margins on the shop side. Let
the customer PDF show the work by vehicle or scope and break out options or extras where useful.

## 2. Scheduling and tracking jobs

**2.5 · Proofs and permission to print.** Show the assigned installer the signed proof directly on
the job with options to open, download and print the PDF. Store which proof version was approved and
pair it with the matching print files. Before releasing work to print, require all applicable items:
the production files, color approval, signed proof and required deposit. Any missing required item
blocks printing. A new proof version must be signed before that version can be released. For work
that does not need printing, use the appropriate workflow for the items sold.

**Box storage and Box Sign.** Keep large production files in Box and signed proofs in Box Sign. Link
the job to its Box files and let the assigned print operator download the released version. Test
whether completed Box Sign proofs can automatically attach to the right job and proof version. If
automatic attachment is impractical for V1, let the Owner attach the signed PDF manually. The
installer can open, download and print the signed PDF from the job either way.

**2.1–2.4 · Job board and schedule.** Moving the HighLevel opportunity into Print Production should
create the shop job from the linked quote. Fleet units need their own dates and status while sharing
a quote and, when needed, one final invoice. Keep Normal, High Priority and Rush. Use one set of job
records for the board, list and calendar. Include separate production and install dates, tentative
and confirmed slots, drop-off and pickup times, onsite addresses, and pickup or shipping for
supply-only work.

**HighLevel handoff.** HighLevel handles leads, the sales stages, texts and emails. Build the quotes
in the shop app and link them to the matching HighLevel opportunities. When a card moves to Print
Production, bring in the linked approved quote and customer details without retyping them. Proposed
handling for Dave to verify: if the same card triggers twice, create only one job or fleet job group.
If the quote link or required details are missing, flag the handoff for review and keep it out of the
print queue. A manual recovery option can cover exceptions.

## 3. Doing the job

**3.5 · Required Pre-Wrap Inspection.** Require a roughly five-minute Pre-Wrap Inspection on the
assigned vehicle job before installation. The installer submits it from a phone. Every inspection
goes to the Owner for internal review, including inspections with no damage. Installation can move
forward after the Owner clears it.

- **Damage, photos and customer approval.** Ask whether there is existing damage. If yes, record each
  affected area and the type of damage, such as chipped paint, dents or scratches. Ask whether photos
  are needed and require an upload if marked required. Save who submitted the inspection and who
  cleared it. The Owner chooses whether to send the inspection to the customer for approval. If the
  Owner requests customer approval, record it before clearing the job.
- **Final quality check** — Confirm graphics, placement and scope against the approved proof. Inspect
  edges, seams, recesses, trim and surface finish. Verify removed hardware was reinstalled where
  applicable, take completion photos, and note any rework or exceptions.
- **Site and supply-only jobs** — Use the relevant checks for site condition, location and placement
  or for proof, quantity and packing.

**3.1–3.4 · Technician view and time.** Keep assigned jobs, schedule, instructions, approved files,
photos and timers easy to reach on a phone. Use simple job time, with one active job timer per person
and support for several people working on the same job. Keep shift time separate for the hours
export. Let employees send work for review and the Owner return it for rework or close it. Detailed
tracking by task can wait.

## 4. Invoicing and payments

**4.2 · Invoice layout, other open invoices and tax.** Use OTC Fleet Graphix as the customer-facing
brand. Show the scope, customer PO, terms, dates, subtotal, tax, payments applied and balance on the
current invoice. If the same customer account has other unpaid invoices, show a small section at the
bottom listing each invoice number and remaining balance. Give each old invoice its own link to open
and pay it. Keep the current invoice amount clear. Accounting will confirm the exact legal biller,
remit-to details, correct QuickBooks company, tax rates, taxable items and exemptions.

**4.3 · Invoice payment page.** Email the invoice as a PDF attachment with a Pay This Invoice link.
The link opens that invoice and its current balance, with a payment action at the bottom. The small
other-invoices section should link to the individual old invoices for that same account. Let
customers pay part or all of a single invoice. Demo the layout on a phone. Have Dave compare Stripe's
hosted option with the required layout and price a custom invoice page if needed. Accounting can
confirm processor fees and how to record offline payments or refunds.

**4.5 · QuickBooks posting.** One-way only. Post finalized invoices, payments and deposits from the
shop app to QuickBooks using the mappings accounting approves. Use the customer account to group
invoices, credits and payments. Apply each payment once and show any failed posting so it can be
retried safely. Keep the customer's balance in the shop app current when payments are recorded there.

**4.1 and 4.4 · Customer accounts and payments.** Closing work can create an invoice draft for Owner
review. Keep job completion, invoicing and payment as separate steps. Keep account-level balances and
credit terms, including COD for first-time customers and Net 30 for approved accounts where
applicable. In V1, customers can pay any part of an individual invoice. Paying the entire account
balance in one transaction across multiple invoices is planned for the later customer portal.

## 5. Materials and ordering

**5.1–5.3 · Materials and vendor orders.** Keep a basic material price list and simple vendor orders
we can export and send ourselves. Use sensible units such as square feet, linear feet, rolls and
each. Detailed tracking of vinyl, ink and laminate used on each job and full inventory can wait.

## 6. Time and payroll

**6.3 · Employee access.** Use two roles, Owner and Technician. The same Technician role can cover
both print operators and installers. They see assigned jobs, schedules, files, signed proofs, photos,
checklists and their own time. The Owner sees finances and settings. Employees can record job time
and submit work for review.

**6.1–6.2 · Hours export.** Keep a simple export of approved employee hours. Let the Owner fix missed
punches and approve the pay period. Keep payroll processing in our current payroll system.

## 7. Reports

**7.2 · What counts as work in progress?** Include approved work where real job work has started,
including billable design, production or installation, and where the related scope has not been
finalized on an invoice. Include work waiting for install, owner review or billing after it has
started. Exclude estimates, scheduled work that hasn't started, canceled work and finalized invoiced
scope. A deposit by itself doesn't make a job started. If some units are billed, show only the
remaining started units. The detail should show unit/scope, stage, approved unbilled value, hours and
due date. The summary can total count, approved unbilled value and hours by stage. Label this an
operational report, with a separate ready-to-bill view for completed work.

**7.1 and 7.3 · Money and time reports.** Keep the money and time reports printable and exportable.
Define revenue by finalized invoice date and collections by payment date, with deposits identifiable
and A/R based on the open balance. Keep clock hours, job hours and approved timesheets distinct. For
Graphix reporting, give us an internal versus external customer-work tag so we can compare the two.

## 8. Setup and moving off FullBay

**8.3 · Moving off FullBay.** Dave handles the trial import, duplicate matching and the final move.
I'll verify active customers, jobs and schedules. Accounting will verify unpaid invoices, credits,
deposits and balances. Preserve the old invoice numbers and link records already in QuickBooks so
they are posted only once. Keep historical records available in FullBay and keep the export
separately. Agree on the actual switch date after the trial import checks out.

**8.1, 8.2 and 8.4 · Scope, devices and email.** Keep V1 graphics-only and browser-based. Build
quotes and create deposit payment links in the shop app. Use HighLevel or our existing email to send
customer messages, quote PDFs, deposit links, and invoice PDFs with the Pay This Invoice link. Use
Box for production files and Box Sign for proofs.

**Customer access now and later.** V1 customers can open the invoice and pay an individual bill
through its link. The later customer portal can let a customer sign in, see their whole account and
pay their entire balance in one transaction. Repair shops, multi-shop switching, automated reminders,
full inventory, ACH links, custom roles and payroll integration can wait.

# What I'd test before switching

- Build a two-van quote in the shop app and link it to HighLevel. Move the card to Print Production,
  repeat the event, and confirm only one fleet job group is created. Schedule the vans separately and
  verify their approved scope can appear on one invoice. Also test a decal or vinyl sale with no
  vehicle.
- Download a large Box print file and print the signed Box Sign proof from an assigned job. Test the
  manual signed-PDF option too. Confirm each missing requirement on its own blocks release to print:
  files, color approval, signed proof or required deposit.
- Create a deposit payment link from a quote and pay it before the job is created. Confirm it follows
  the job and is applied once to the final invoice. Open Pay This Invoice from the email, pay part of
  the bill, and check the remaining account balance. Open an older unpaid invoice from the bottom
  section. Retry QuickBooks posting and confirm the invoice and payments appear once.
- Submit a clean inspection and one with damage. Confirm both go to the Owner, neither is sent to the
  customer automatically, and installation waits for clearance. Test the option for the Owner to
  request customer approval.
